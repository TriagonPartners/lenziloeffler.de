From Kaufbeuren to Chicago

I grew up in Kaufbeuren, a small town south of Munich. As a competitive Taekwondo athlete, I competed nationally and internationally in regional squads, coming close to national-team level toward the end of high school. It gave me an early perspective on talent, performance — and the importance of the environment around an athlete.

The U.S. was always the dream. After graduating in 2010, I made it happen differently: a year in Chicago, volunteering full-time at a homeless shelter while living in one of the city’s most vibrant neighborhoods — and travelling the country along the way, from New York to California and Miami, with L.A., Santa Monica and Malibu among the places I had long wanted to experience.

Sports came with it: I committed to the Bears at the beginning of the 2006 season — initially drawn in by Devin Hester, with no idea they would make it to the Super Bowl. The Bulls had been on my radar since the Michael Jordan era, and my year in the U.S. happened to coincide with two moments I won’t forget: Derrick Rose’s MVP season, which I experienced at the United Center, and Dirk Nowitzki winning the championship.

Munich — Learning & Entering Technology

I studied American Studies and Philosophy at LMU Munich while gaining my first professional experience at Munich City Council. My connection to Chicago continued as I helped prepare and mentor future volunteer cohorts for a structured educational service abroad, supporting them before departure and after their return.

Research for my B.A. thesis on U.S. foreign policy and diplomatic history later took me to New York and Washington, D.C., while my work at Munich City Council gave me an early view into public services, research and policy consulting. Today, I am finishing my Master’s alongside building my own businesses and ventures, exploring technology, culture, sports and high performance — and their connections to business and everyday life. With an MBA already in planning, academic learning will continue to complement my professional path. Connecting theory with practical application has always been important to me.

Augsburg — Balance & Building from Scratch

Augsburg had always been close to home, and so had FC Augsburg. When the club reached the Bundesliga, a season ticket followed. My career in the technology ecosystem had already started in Munich, where strong results and the trust that came with them opened up an internal opportunity in Augsburg. What started as an idea became reality surprisingly fast. I actively pursued the opportunity, moved to Augsburg and continued within an already well-running setup, while my client work increasingly expanded beyond Munich across Germany.

Augsburg became a good base for that next phase: close to my home region of Bavarian Swabia, with short distances, nature and a strong sense of history. Its Renaissance heritage and Mediterranean touches give the city its own character, while Munich remains close and frequent business travel kept the wider professional world within reach.

Professionally, I moved from business development in technology consulting to executive search in the software industry. In 2026, I brought those two sides together and started building my own businesses and ventures in tech & leadership advisory, leveraging AI for automation, productivity and performance enhancement while keeping human judgment central to people and leadership decisions.

Leaving the structure of employment and building independently changed the operating context. It requires focus, resilience and responsibility, but also creates the freedom to decide what to build, how to work and where to invest energy. For me, that has made clarity, direction and progress more practical than conceptual.

Motorsport — Building Around Performance

Sport never really went away. I still play basketball and go for a run — and more recently discovered motorsport through supporting a young driver from Munich competing in a Formula 3 racing series.

It started with a chance conversation at a business reunion. Less than six weeks later, Monza was on the calendar.

The sporting potential was there, but the resources to compete were not. Bringing people and businesses together around that potential helped make the race weekend possible — and put me inside the team for the full experience: paddock and garages, evening track walks, and seeing first-hand how driver, engineers, technology, preparation and external conditions interact.

He went on to win at Monza — his third consecutive victory at Formula 1 circuits after the Red Bull Ring and Imola, taking him back to the top of the Austria Formula Cup standings.

That got me hooked. Not only on motorsport, but on what surrounds performance: the people, technology, partnerships and structures that allow talent to progress.

It also brought me back to my own experience as a young athlete. Today, I want to combine what I have learned across competitive sports, business and volunteering to support ambitious young athletes in building that wider performance ecosystem — and taking the next step when talent alone isn’t enough.

Open to advisory inquiries, potential partnerships and conversations around athlete development.
