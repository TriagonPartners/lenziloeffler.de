Formula 3 at Sixteen

Nicolas „Lenzi“ Loeffler, 16, competes in a Dallara F312 under number 91 in national and regional Formula 3 competition across Austria, Italy and the FIA Central European Zone. In his debut season at this level, he currently leads four class standings.

With the season entering its final stages, he has competed in six of ten race weekends, recording eleven class wins, two outright podiums and fastest laps in seven of his twelve races — a record that reflects both consistent pace and steady development across the year.

His path into single-seaters began at eleven. After initial Formula car experience and structured testing in Formula Renault machinery, 2025 marked his first full season of racing in the Drexler Formula Cup. The move into the Dallara F312 followed in 2026, bringing more power, downforce and technical complexity, and providing the next step in his progression as a driver.

Monza: A Key Moment

An opening-round victory at the Red Bull Ring, followed by a pole-to-win performance at Imola, provided early evidence that the pace could be repeated across different circuits and conditions.

Technical issues around the Grobnik round then prompted a reassessment of the season approach. The response was to strengthen the technical and team environment, sharpen preparation and place greater emphasis on the structures needed for continued improvement.

A first commercial partnership helped make the Monza round possible, where the strengthened technical and team setup provided the platform to build on the pace already demonstrated earlier in the season.

The Car, the Team, the Craft

Nicolas has raced the Dallara F312 throughout 2026: 245 hp at 460 kilograms, sequential six-speed paddle shift and speeds of up to 260 km/h — a generation of Formula 3 machinery also raced by Max Verstappen at sixteen.

With Franz Wöss Racing from Monza onward, he recorded three class wins and one second place across the parallel Austria Formel Cup and TopJet F2000 classifications, taking the lead in both standings.

At the Slovakia Ring, he carried that momentum forward with a charge from the pit lane to third overall, alongside class victories in both the Austria Formel Cup and FIA Central European Zone D2F3. Brno in September and the season finale at Mugello in October now provide the final two opportunities to build on that progress and close out his first year in Formula 3 machinery on a strong trajectory.

Building Towards 2027

The immediate priority is to finish the 2026 season at the same level: maintain pace, deliver consistently across the final two race weekends and continue developing within the team, both technically and as a driver. The goal is to protect the current positions and extract the strongest possible performances through to the season finale.

That will provide the basis for the next stage of sporting development and the preparation for 2027. Potential routes include the established F4 Spanish Championship, the returning F4 German Championship on the DTM platform — last won in 2022 by Kimi Antonelli with PREMA Racing — and Eurocup-3 as possible next steps in the progression.

We are currently building a focused commercial partnership platform around Nicolas’ racing programme, with opportunities spanning brand visibility, media and content activation, and race-weekend hospitality. The aim is to create partnerships that are professionally structured, relevant to the partner and able to grow with his sporting development.

If you’re interested in supporting an ambitious young single-seater driver, we look forward to exploring long-term partnership opportunities that bring sporting ambition and business interests together.
